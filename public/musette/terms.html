2. What Musette does

Musette reads your training and recovery from Apple Health, takes the event you're training for, and suggests one workout a day from a library of Zwift workouts built into the app, with a short reason. Its Week tab plans when to ride over the coming days, using a weather forecast to suggest indoor or outdoor days, and can add those sessions to your calendar.

Picks come from fixed rules applied to the data on your iPhone. They're suggestions to help you decide, not a coaching plan or a medical assessment.

3. Train safely

  • Musette's picks are training suggestions, not medical advice. Musette can't see how you feel, and it isn't a substitute for a doctor or a qualified coach.
  • Talk to a doctor before you start or step up training, especially if you have a heart or breathing condition, high blood pressure or diabetes, are pregnant, are recovering from illness or injury, or haven't exercised for a while.
  • Stop and get medical help if you have chest pain, feel faint or dizzy, or are unusually short of breath while you ride. Rest when you're unwell or injured, whatever Musette suggests.
  • Picks rely on the data in Apple Health and the numbers you enter, which can be missing or wrong, for example a ride recorded without heart rate or a night when you didn't wear your watch. You decide whether to train and how hard.

4. Riding outside

  • Forecasts can be wrong. An "outdoor" day in Musette is a suggestion: check the weather and the roads before you set off.
  • Don't use Musette while you're riding. Follow the Highway Code and ride within your ability and the conditions.
