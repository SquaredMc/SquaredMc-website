Privacy Policy

Last updated: 6 November 2026  ·  Applies to the Musette app for iPhone and iPad and these web pages.

The short version

  • There's no account and no Musette server. We never receive your Apple Health data, your plan or your daily picks.
  • Your Apple Health data stays on each of your devices. Your plan (your goal, training week, rider numbers, forecast location, daily picks and past events) syncs between your iPhone and iPad through your own private iCloud.
  • For the weather forecast, Musette sends Apple an approximate location, rounded to about a kilometre.
  • If you connect your calendar, Musette reads only when you're busy. The sessions you add go in a Training calendar that syncs with your calendar account like your other calendars.
  • No ads, no analytics and no tracking.

1. Who we are

Musette is an app for iPhone and iPad that suggests which Zwift workout to ride today, with the reason, based on your training and recovery in Apple Health and the event you're training for. It's built by SQUAREDMC LTD ("we", "us"), a company registered in England and Wales with company number 16175464, whose registered office is at 17 Poole Road, Epsom, KT19 9SQ.

This policy explains what information the Musette app and these web pages use, why, and your rights. Most of it stays on your devices or in your own iCloud, so we don't hold it. For the little we do hold, described in sections 11 and 14, we're the controller. If you have any questions, email us at hello@squaredmc.com.

2. Apple Health

If you allow it, Musette reads these from Apple Health: workouts, heart rate, cycling power, cycling distance, resting heart rate, heart rate variability, sleep, weight and your date of birth.

  • What it's for. Musette uses them to work out your recent training load, how recovered you look, and which workout fits today. Your weight is used to work out the power your event target needs. Your date of birth gives your age, which is used only to estimate your maximum heart rate if you haven't entered it.
  • Read only. Musette never writes to Apple Health.
  • In the background. Musette also refreshes in the background, when iOS tells it new workouts, sleep or heart data have arrived and early each morning, so today's pick, the widget and the forecast are up to date when you look.
  • History. To show how your training has gone over time and to compare one event's build with the last, Musette reads back through your older workouts and resting heart rate and keeps a summary of them in a file on that device. The file isn't synced and isn't included in device backups. If it's lost, Musette reads it again from Apple Health.
  • On each device. Your Health data is used on the device that reads it. Musette doesn't send it to us or store it in iCloud, and it isn't used for advertising. On an iPad, Musette reads the Health data that Apple's own Health sync brings to it. Two things based on your Health data can leave your device: the reason line saved with each daily pick, which syncs through your iCloud (section 3), and the reason for a session you add to your calendar (section 5).

You can change what Musette can read at any time in the Health app: tap your profile picture, then Apps under Privacy, then Musette.

3. Your plan and iCloud

If you're signed in to iCloud, Musette keeps your plan in the private database of your own iCloud account, so your iPhone and iPad show the same plan and you only set Musette up once. Your plan is:

  • your goal: an event's name, date and type, or "just keep me fit". For an event chosen from Musette's list, it also holds the route, your target time and planned stops, and the event's figures when you chose it;
  • your training week, your rider numbers (FTP, maximum heart rate and threshold heart rate) and the place for your weather forecast;
  • your daily picks for the last year, each with its workout, alternative and reason, and which workouts you've been given recently; and
  • your past events: each one's name, date and type and, if you answer "How did it go?", how it went and any note you write.

Your Health data isn't part of your plan. A pick's reason can mention something like a low heart rate variability or short sleep, but the numbers behind it aren't stored. Your weight, and the fitness, fatigue, power and heart rate figures Musette works out, are never stored in iCloud.

  • Who can see it. Only you, through your Apple Account. We can't see it. Apple encrypts it, and if you've turned on Advanced Data Protection for iCloud, it's end-to-end encrypted. Apple's iCloud terms and privacy policy apply.
  • Turning it off. To stop syncing, open the Settings app, tap your name, then iCloud, and turn Musette off. Musette then keeps your plan on that device only. If you're not signed in to iCloud, it does that anyway.
  • After you delete the app. Deleting Musette doesn't remove your plan from iCloud, so it comes back if you reinstall. To remove it, delete Musette's data from your iCloud storage in the Settings app (your name, then iCloud), or email us and we'll help.

If Apple Health has no weight for you, Musette asks you to type it in. A weight you type is kept only on that device and isn't synced.

4. Location and weather

The Week tab can mark which days suit riding outside, using a weather forecast. For that it needs a location, which you choose to give.

  • Approximate and occasional. The first time you open the Week tab, Musette asks for location access "While Using the App". If you allow it, Musette takes a single reading and rounds it to two decimal places, about a kilometre, and keeps that rounded position as the place for your forecast. It's kept with your plan, so it syncs through your iCloud (section 3). You can take a new reading later with Use my approximate location. Musette doesn't follow where you go, and it never asks for "Always" access.
  • Or a town. You can type a town instead. The text you type goes to Apple to find the place.
  • What Apple receives. To fetch the forecast, Musette sends the rounded position to Apple Weather every few hours, including when it refreshes in the background. To show the town's name, it may also send the rounded position to Apple's place lookup service. It downloads Apple Weather's logo to show with the forecast, without your location. Apple handles these requests under its own privacy policy. Nothing is sent to us.

You can remove the location at any time on the Week tab: tap Change location, then Forget the forecast location. You can turn off location access in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services → Musette. Without a location the week loses its indoor and outdoor labels, and everything else keeps working. Weather data is provided by Apple Weather.

5. Calendar

Connecting your calendar is optional, and Musette works fully without it. If you connect it, iOS asks you to give Musette full access to your calendars. Calendar access is set up on each device separately.

  • What Musette reads. Only the start and end times of events over the coming days, to find when you're busy. It doesn't read event titles, notes or locations. For invitations, it checks only whether you've declined, so declined ones don't count. Events marked free and all-day events are ignored too.
  • What Musette writes. When you tap Add to calendar, Musette creates a calendar called "Training" and adds the training sessions for the coming days to it: the time, a short title such as "Indoor ride · 60 min" or, on the day, the workout's name, and a note with the reason for the session. The reason can mention your event and how recovered you look, such as short sleep or a low heart rate variability, so it goes wherever your calendar syncs. Once you've added sessions, Musette keeps today's event up to date with the day's pick, including when it refreshes in the background. It updates or removes only the events it added, and never changes your other events.
  • Where that calendar lives. Musette creates the Training calendar in your default calendar account, or, if it can't, in another calendar account or on that device only. If it's in iCloud, Google or another online account, the Training calendar and its events sync to it like your other calendars, and that provider's privacy policy applies to them. No calendar data is sent to us, and none is part of your plan.

Deleting Musette doesn't delete the Training calendar. You can delete it in the Calendar app. You can turn off calendar access in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Calendars → Musette.

6. Notifications

Musette can send two kinds of notification, and only if you allow them:

  • Weekly. If you turn on Remind me Sundays at 18:00 on the Week tab, a notification each Sunday says your week is ready.
  • Event changes. If you set a goal from Musette's event list and allow notifications, Musette tells you once when something important about that event changes, such as its route being confirmed, its date moving or the event being cancelled.

Both are scheduled by the app on your device. They don't come from a server. You can turn them off in iOS Settings → Notifications → Musette, and the weekly one on the Week tab.

7. Events and links

Musette's list of events is built into the app, and Musette doesn't contact any server to fetch it. An event's page can link to the organiser's website. If you tap it, the site opens in your browser, and the organiser's own privacy policy applies there.

8. The widget

The app and its Home Screen widget share a storage area on your device that only they can use. The widget shows today's pick and your event countdown from it.

9. What's stored on each device

Musette keeps these on each device it's installed on:

  • a copy of your plan (section 3), which is the only part that syncs;
  • a weight you've typed, and whether Health, location, calendar and notifications are set up on that device;
  • the coming days' training slots, and which calendar events Musette added;
  • the latest forecast;
  • the summary of your older workouts and resting heart rate used by History (section 2);
  • a technical log of what the app did, such as when it refreshed, including some of the numbers it worked from, such as your heart rate numbers, FTP and event date;
  • crash and hang reports, which Apple's MetricKit gives the app. Musette keeps the newest 50 on the device and leaves them out of backups; and
  • if the diagnostics screen in Settings is opened, a file listing your recent workouts' times, durations, distances, power and heart rate, used to check the app's calculations.

None of this is sent to us unless you choose to share it (section 11). Apart from the History summary and the crash reports, it's included in your device's backups to iCloud or a computer if you make them. Deleting Musette deletes all of it from that device, apart from the Training calendar described in section 5. Your plan stays in your iCloud, as section 3 explains.

10. Zwift

Musette suggests workouts from Zwift's library. Its Zwift button opens the Zwift app, or Zwift Companion if that's what you have, or Zwift's page on the App Store if neither is installed. Nothing is sent to Zwift, and Musette doesn't connect to your Zwift account. Zwift's own privacy policy applies when you use Zwift. Musette isn't affiliated with or endorsed by Zwift, Inc.

11. The beta

While Musette is in beta, we invite testers ourselves through Apple's TestFlight. If you're one of them:

  • we hold the name and email address we used to invite you, in our Apple developer account;
  • TestFlight tells us when you install the beta and how often it's used, and shares crash reports with us, along with your device model and iOS version;
  • if you send feedback through TestFlight, we receive what you write and any screenshot you attach; and
  • beta builds have a Diagnostics screen in Settings. If you use it to share the crash reports and the technical log described in section 9, we receive what you send. Nothing is sent unless you choose to.

We use this only to run the beta and improve Musette. We remove you from TestFlight when the beta ends or if you ask us to. Apple's TestFlight terms and privacy policy also apply.

12. Usage, analytics and crash data

Musette doesn't include any analytics, advertising or third-party crash-reporting tools. Apart from the beta, described in section 11, the only crash reports we see come from Apple: if you've chosen to share analytics with app developers in your device's settings, Apple may share crash reports and usage statistics with us, which don't identify you. If we ever add analytics, we'll update this policy before that collection starts.

13. What we don't collect

There's no account, so we don't collect your name, email address or phone number (unless you're a beta tester or you email us). Musette doesn't access your contacts, photos, camera or microphone. It doesn't show ads, track you across other companies' apps and websites, or sell or share your information.

If you email us, we'll have your email address and whatever you include, and we'll use them only to reply to you.

15. Legal basis and your rights

Under UK data protection law (the UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018), we rely on our legitimate interests to hold beta testers' details, TestFlight reports and any diagnostics testers share, so we can run the beta, and to reply when you email us. Your Health data, location, calendar and the plan in your iCloud never reach us, so we don't process them.

You have the right to access, correct or delete your personal data, to object to or restrict how we use it, and to data portability. For beta testers we can give you, correct or delete what we hold. Everything else lives on your devices and in your own iCloud, where you control it, as sections 3 and 9 describe.

To make a request, email hello@squaredmc.com. If you're unhappy with how we've handled your information, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk or on 0303 123 1113.

16. Children

Musette isn't aimed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect information from them. If you think a child has sent us information, contact us and we'll delete it.
